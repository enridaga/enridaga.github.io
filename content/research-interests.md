## Knowledge Graph Engineering

Design, construction, and curation of knowledge graphs at scale — including the development of reusable ontologies, design patterns, and engineering pipelines. A central contribution in this area is the **façade-based data access** approach to knowledge graph construction: rather than requiring custom ETL pipelines for each data source, a façade provides a uniform abstraction over heterogeneous formats and APIs, enabling them to be queried directly as if they were RDF. This idea is formalised in **Facade-X**, a meta-model that implements the façade pattern by overriding the SPARQL SERVICE operator, and realised in **[SPARQL Anything](https://sparql.anything.cc)**, an open-source engine that puts it into practice across formats including JSON, CSV, XML, HTML, spreadsheets, and more.

The long-term standardisation of this approach is pursued through the **[W3C Data Façades Community Group](https://www.w3.org/groups/cg/facade-x/)**, of which Enrico is a founding member and Chair (from September 2025). The group's goal is to foster stability and broader industry adoption of façade-based access to heterogeneous web data within the RDF ecosystem.

## Data Life-cycles, Policies, and Distributed Infrastructures

How data is created, shared, transformed, and deployed across distributed systems — with attention to provenance, quality, interoperability, and rights management. This includes research into machine-readable representations of licences, digital rights, and access conditions, enabling automated compliance reasoning in data pipelines and knowledge graph applications. Applied in the context of institutional open data (OU Open Knowledge Graph), the SPICE Linked Data Hub — a platform developed within the EU H2020 SPICE project to store, query, and reason over citizen - contributed cultural heritage annotations — and broader cross-organisational data infrastructures.

## Humanities Knowledge Graphs

A sustained research programme applying knowledge graph methods to the specific challenges of humanities scholarship — where sources are heterogeneous, multilingual, incomplete, and deeply contextual. This work spans the full pipeline from source material to usable knowledge infrastructure: extracting structured information from digitised historical documents and music scores, modelling complex cultural phenomena (performances, listening experiences, social encounters between musicians), and building open platforms that support both scholarly inquiry and citizen engagement.

Key projects include **[Polifonia](http://www.polifonia-project.eu)** (EU H2020, 2021–2024), in which Enrico served as Principal Investigator (OU) and Technical Director, building a network of interconnected knowledge graphs covering European musical heritage; the **[Listening Experience Database (LED)](https://led.kmi.open.ac.uk)** (AHRC), which applied NLP and linked data to the large-scale extraction of evidence about music listening from historical texts; and the **[Musical Meetups Knowledge Graph (MMKG)](https://polifonia.kmi.open.ac.uk/meetups/)**, a resource for historical social network analysis reconstructed from documentary evidence of encounters between musicians.

The interplay between knowledge graphs and generative AI — including the opportunities and risks that large language models introduce for humanities data — is an active and growing research thread, explored in the invited seminars *"Humanities Knowledge Graphs: opportunities and challenges with gen-AI"* at the University of Turin (December 2025) and at the Digital Humanities Research Centre of the University of Groningen (February 2026).

Enrico has contributed to building the research community in this space as co-chair of the [WHiSe workshop series](https://whise.cc/2020/) (Humanities in the Semantic Web, co-located with ESWC/ISWC, 2016–2020) and as co-chair of the [DARIAH AI and Music (AIM) working group](https://ai-music.org) (2019–2024).

## Application Domains

- **Cultural Heritage** — knowledge graph methods applied to musical heritage ([Polifonia](http://www.polifonia-project.eu)), citizen curation of museum collections ([SPICE](https://spice-h2020.eu)), and the history of music listening ([LED](https://led.kmi.open.ac.uk)).
- **Digital Humanities & Social Sciences** — computational approaches to humanities scholarship (see [Humanities Knowledge Graphs](#humanities-knowledge-graphs) above), and knowledge graph approaches to climate misinformation surveillance ([ClimateSense](https://climatesense-project.eu)).
- **Smart Cities & Healthcare** — urban data integration and open data infrastructure ([MK:Smart](https://www.mksmart.org), [MK:5G](https://kmi.open.ac.uk/projects/name/mk-5g)). Knowledge-driven approaches to clinical decision support, including representing and reasoning over health condition evolution for emergency response (led by PhD student Alba Morales Tirado), and visual intelligence for service robotics in care environments (Agnese Chiatti).
